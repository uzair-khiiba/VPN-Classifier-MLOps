## How to read this

- **CV F1** comes from 5-fold cross-validation on training IPs, and **Val F1** from one validation split. They were available when models were chosen. **Test** columns weren't, and only report the result.
- **The deployed model and the best experimental model are different.** The experimental model scores higher, but it stays undeployed until someone approves it through `experiments.promote_model`.
- **Class imbalance.** Only 1.95% of test IPs are VPN/proxy, so labelling everything "not VPN" scores 98.05% accuracy with F1 0 and PR-AUC about 0.019. That's why accuracy isn't used here, and why ROC-AUC can look excellent while F1 is modest.
- **Ludwig's comparison isn't like for like.** It trained on 94,885 IPs rather than 118,607, used one validation split rather than 5 folds, and optimised ROC-AUC in an effectively random search. This shows how one configuration did on one split. It doesn't show that Ludwig, or neural networks, are worse in general.
- **The README's earlier Ludwig result (test F1 0.711)** has no saved run or predictions, so it's excluded.

## Choosing a search objective for Ludwig

Across 72 runs on the same folds, using cross-validation only:

| Metric | Spearman with CV F1 | Of the top 10 by F1, also top 10 by this metric | CV F1 of the run it would pick |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| PR-AUC | 0.926 | 9 | 0.7905 |
| ROC-AUC | 0.631 | 7 | 0.7849 |

Validation PR-AUC as the search objective. Fixed-threshold F1 was rejected: Ludwig would measure it at 0.5, while the project tunes the threshold afterwards, and class weights change what 0.5 means from trial to trial. PR-AUC needs no threshold and focuses on the rare class. ROC-AUC stays as a second recorded metric.
